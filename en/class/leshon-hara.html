Leshon Hara

In Jewish thought, lashon hara means harmful speech. It refers to speaking negatively about another person, spreading private information, or causing damage through words. The Jewish tradition views speech as a powerful moral force. A person may use words to connect, encourage, and teach, but speech can also wound, divide, and defame. For that reason, lashon hara is treated as a serious spiritual wrongdoing.

What the Jewish Tradition Teaches

The prohibition of lashon hara is not limited to obvious lies. Even if a statement is true, speaking about another person in a damaging way can still be wrong when the speech is unnecessary, cruel, or meant to hurt. Jewish ethics emphasize restraint, discretion, and the value of protecting a person’s dignity.

The Torah and the Sages teach that words have the power to corrupt relationships and create a climate of suspicion. Therefore, the moral discipline of speech is one of the foundations of a just and holy life. A Jew is taught not only to avoid falsehood, but also to avoid careless or destructive speech.

Why It Matters

Lashon hara damages trust. It strips away the honor of another person and often causes long-term emotional harm. Because words can travel far beyond the speaker’s intention, Jewish tradition treats speech with seriousness and caution. Even a seemingly small comment can become a source of division.

The deeper lesson is that speech should be used for truth, correction, and strengthening, not for humiliation or gossip. The moral goal is not only to avoid evil speech, but to cultivate a life of integrity, compassion, and careful judgment.

Practical Jewish Guidance

In practice, a person should pause before speaking about others. Ask whether the information is necessary, whether it is true, whether it is helpful, and whether it honors the dignity of the person. If there is no constructive reason to speak, silence is often the more righteous path.

Jewish ethics teach that the power of speech is sacred. When a person restrains harmful words and chooses kindness instead, they strengthen both personal character and the moral health of their community.
