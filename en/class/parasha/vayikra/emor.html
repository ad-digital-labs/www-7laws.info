Emor · Leviticus 21:1–24:23

Parasha Emor

Laws for the priests, the sacred calendar of Jewish holidays, and the Omer count.

Overview

Parasha Emor covers the special laws governing the Kohanim (priests), the requirements for animals brought as offerings, and the complete calendar of Jewish holidays — Shabbat, Passover, Shavuot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, and Sukkot.

The Story

Priests must avoid ritual impurity and physical blemishes that would disqualify them from service. Animals offered must be without blemish. The holidays are listed as "appointed times of God" — Shabbat weekly, Passover in Nissan, the Omer count of 49 days, Shavuot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, and Sukkot. The parasha also describes the showbread in the Tabernacle and concludes with the case of a man who blasphemed God's name — establishing the death penalty for blasphemy.

Significance for Noahides

The Jewish holidays are described as "appointed times of God" — moments in the annual cycle when the divine and human meet in a special way. The prohibition of blasphemy, which concludes this parasha, is one of the Seven Noahide Laws. The case is presented as applying to both Israelites and strangers — underscoring its universal nature.
