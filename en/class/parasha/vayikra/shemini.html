Shemini · Leviticus 9:1–11:47

Parasha Shemini

The glory of God appears, two priests die for offering strange fire, and the kosher laws are given.

Overview

Parasha Shemini — "the eighth" — describes the climactic eighth day of the Tabernacle inauguration, when God's glory finally appears before all the people. It is also the parasha of one of the Torah's most sobering events: the sudden death of Aaron's two sons, Nadav and Avihu.

The Story

On the eighth day, Aaron and his sons perform the inaugural service. Fire comes forth from God and consumes the offerings — the people shout and fall on their faces. Then Nadav and Avihu offer "strange fire" that God had not commanded. Fire comes from God and kills them. Aaron is silent. Moses explains: "Through those near to Me I will be sanctified." The laws of kosher animals, fish, birds, and insects follow — the dietary laws that distinguish Israel and teach the sanctity of the body.

Significance for Noahides

The death of Nadav and Avihu teaches that even well-intentioned religious innovation can be dangerous when it departs from divine instruction. The kosher laws, while not binding on Noahides, teach the principle that what we eat affects who we are — that the body is a vessel for the soul and deserves to be treated with care and intentionality.
