Bechukotai · Leviticus 26:3–27:34

Parasha Bechukotai

Blessings for obedience, consequences for disobedience, and the unbreakable covenant.

Overview

Parasha Bechukotai closes the Book of Vayikra with the Tochachah — the great passage of blessings and rebukes. If Israel follows God's laws, they will receive abundant blessing. If they reject them, they will face escalating consequences. Yet the parasha ends with an unconditional promise: God will never completely abandon Israel or break His covenant with them.

The Story

The blessings: rain in its season, abundant harvests, peace in the land, victory over enemies, and God's presence dwelling among the people. The rebukes escalate in seven stages if Israel rejects the covenant — disease, drought, war, exile, and desolation. Yet even in exile, if Israel confesses and humbles itself, God will remember His covenant with the patriarchs and restore them. The book closes with laws of vows and dedications to the Tabernacle.

Significance for Noahides

The Tochachah teaches the principle of divine accountability — that moral choices have consequences, both individually and collectively. The unconditional promise at the end — that God will never utterly reject His people — reflects the nature of the divine covenant: it is not a contract that can be voided by human failure, but a relationship sustained by divine love and faithfulness.
