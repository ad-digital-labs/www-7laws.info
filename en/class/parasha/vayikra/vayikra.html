Vayikra · Leviticus 1:1–5:26

Parasha Vayikra

God calls to Moses and teaches the laws of the burnt offering, meal offering, and peace offering.

Overview

Parasha Vayikra opens the Book of Leviticus with God calling to Moses from the Tabernacle and teaching the laws of the various sacrificial offerings. The Hebrew word for offering — korban — comes from the root meaning "to draw near," expressing the spiritual purpose of the sacrificial service.

The Story

God describes the burnt offering (olah), the meal offering (mincha), the peace offering (shelamim), the sin offering (chatat), and the guilt offering (asham). Each type of offering serves a different spiritual purpose — expressing devotion, gratitude, atonement, or the repair of a specific wrong. The laws apply to both the community and the individual, and even to the High Priest and the king.

Significance for Noahides

The sacrificial system teaches that human beings have an innate need to express their relationship with God through concrete action. Though Noahides are not obligated in the Temple service, the underlying principles — gratitude, atonement, and drawing near to God — are universal. The Talmud records that Noah himself offered sacrifices after the Flood, establishing this as a universal human practice.
