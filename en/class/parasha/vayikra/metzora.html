Metzora · Leviticus 14:1–15:33

Parasha Metzora

The purification process for tzaraat afflictions and the laws of the afflicted house.

Overview

Parasha Metzora describes the elaborate purification ritual for a person healed of tzaraat, the laws of a house afflicted with tzaraat, and various laws of bodily impurity. The purification process involves two birds, cedar wood, crimson thread, and hyssop — a rich symbolic ceremony of renewal.

The Story

When a person is healed of tzaraat, the priest performs a purification ritual outside the camp involving two birds — one slaughtered over fresh water, one dipped in the blood and released alive. The person then shaves, immerses, and after seven days is fully purified. A house with tzaraat-like afflictions is examined by the priest; if the affliction spreads, the stones are removed and replaced. If it persists, the house is demolished. The parasha concludes with laws of bodily discharges and their purification.

Significance for Noahides

The two birds of the purification ritual are interpreted as representing the two aspects of speech: one bird is slaughtered — representing harmful speech that must be silenced — and one is released — representing the power of speech used for good, set free to soar. The entire parasha teaches that purification is a process, not an instant event, and that returning to wholeness requires time, ritual, and intention.
