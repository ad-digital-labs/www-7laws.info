Beshalach · Exodus 13:17–17:16

Parasha Beshalach

The sea splits, Israel sings, and God begins to sustain His people in the wilderness.

Overview

Parasha Beshalach contains the most dramatic miracle of the Exodus: the splitting of the Red Sea. It also introduces the manna — the miraculous bread from heaven — and the first battle Israel fights as a free nation. It is a parasha of faith tested and confirmed.

The Story

Pharaoh regrets letting Israel go and pursues them with his army. Israel is trapped between the sea and the Egyptians. The people cry out in fear; Moses tells them: "Do not be afraid. Stand firm and see the salvation of God." God parts the sea; Israel crosses on dry land. The Egyptians follow and are drowned when the waters return. Moses and Israel sing the Song of the Sea — one of the oldest and most beautiful poems in human literature. Miriam leads the women in song and dance.

In the wilderness, the people complain of thirst and hunger. God provides manna — a fine, flake-like substance that appears each morning — and quail. The manna comes with instructions: gather a double portion on Friday; none will fall on Shabbat. Some disobey; Moses rebukes them. Israel fights Amalek; as long as Moses holds up his hands, Israel prevails.

Significance for Noahides

The Song of the Sea is the first great hymn of praise in the Torah — a spontaneous outpouring of gratitude from an entire nation. The manna teaches complete dependence on God and the sanctity of Shabbat. The battle with Amalek — the archetype of evil that attacks the weak — teaches that the fight against moral corruption requires both human effort and divine support.
