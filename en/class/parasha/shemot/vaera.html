Vaera · Exodus 6:2–9:35

Parasha Vaera

God reveals His name, and the first seven plagues strike Egypt.

Overview

Parasha Vaera opens with God's revelation of His name — YHVH — and His promise of redemption through four expressions of deliverance. It then describes the first seven of the ten plagues, each one a direct assault on an Egyptian deity and a demonstration of God's absolute sovereignty.

The Story

God tells Moses He appeared to the patriarchs as El Shaddai but was not known by His name YHVH. He reaffirms the covenant and promises to redeem Israel with an outstretched arm and great judgments. Moses and Aaron go to Pharaoh; Aaron's staff becomes a serpent, but Pharaoh's magicians replicate the sign. The plagues begin: blood, frogs, lice (which the magicians cannot replicate — "This is the finger of God"), wild beasts, pestilence on livestock, boils. After each plague, Pharaoh hardens his heart and refuses to let Israel go.

Significance for Noahides

The plagues are not random acts of destruction — they are a systematic dismantling of Egyptian idolatry. The Nile was a god; it became blood. Frogs were sacred; they became a plague. Each plague declared: there is only one God, and He is the God of Israel. For Noahides, the Exodus is the foundational event that demonstrates God's active involvement in history and His commitment to justice and the liberation of the oppressed.
