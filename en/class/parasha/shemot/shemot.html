Shemot · Exodus 1:1–6:1

Parasha Shemot

A new king, a hidden child, a burning bush — the redeemer of Israel is called.

Overview

Parasha Shemot opens the Book of Exodus with the enslavement of Israel and the birth of Moses — the greatest prophet who ever lived. It culminates in God's revelation at the burning bush and Moses' first confrontation with Pharaoh.

The Story

A new Pharaoh arises who did not know Joseph. He enslaves the Israelites and orders the killing of all newborn Hebrew boys. A Levite woman hides her son for three months, then places him in a basket on the Nile. Pharaoh's daughter finds him and raises him as her own, naming him Moses. Moses grows up, sees an Egyptian beating a Hebrew, and kills the Egyptian. He flees to Midian, where he marries Zipporah and tends the flocks of her father Jethro.

At Mount Horeb, Moses sees a bush burning but not consumed. God calls to him from the bush, identifies Himself as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and commissions Moses to lead Israel out of Egypt. Moses protests his inadequacy; God gives him signs and sends his brother Aaron to help. Moses returns to Egypt. His first audience with Pharaoh ends badly — Pharaoh increases the Israelites' workload. The people blame Moses. God reassures him: the redemption is coming.

Significance for Noahides

The burning bush — fire that burns but does not consume — is a symbol of the divine presence: powerful, transforming, yet not destructive to what is holy. God's self-identification as the God of the patriarchs establishes the continuity of the covenant. Moses' reluctance and God's patient insistence model the dynamic of divine calling: God chooses the humble, not the proud.
