Tetzaveh · Exodus 27:20–30:10

Parasha Tetzaveh

God designs the sacred garments of the High Priest and consecrates Aaron and his sons.

Overview

Parasha Tetzaveh continues the Tabernacle instructions with the design of the priestly garments — the ephod, breastplate, robe, tunic, turban, and sash — and the seven-day inauguration ceremony for Aaron and his sons as priests.

The Story

The High Priest breastplate contains twelve precious stones engraved with the names of the twelve tribes — he carries all of Israel over his heart when he enters the sanctuary. The inauguration involves anointing, special offerings, and a week of consecration. The parasha ends with the golden incense altar.

Significance for Noahides

The priestly service teaches that approaching the divine requires preparation, purity, and proper intention. The garments of the High Priest — described as being "for honor and for beauty" — teach that the service of God deserves our finest effort and most careful attention.
