Ki Tisa · Exodus 30:11–34:35

Parasha Ki Tisa

Israel's greatest sin, Moses' greatest prayer, and God's greatest act of forgiveness.

Overview

Parasha Ki Tisa contains the most dramatic crisis in Israel's history: the Golden Calf. While Moses is on the mountain receiving the Torah, the people grow impatient and demand Aaron make them a god. God tells Moses what has happened and proposes to destroy Israel and start over with Moses. Moses intercedes powerfully, and God relents.

The Story

Moses descends, sees the calf, and shatters the tablets in anguish. He grinds the calf to powder and makes the people drink it. The Levites rally to Moses and execute the ringleaders. Moses returns to God and pleads for forgiveness — offering to be erased from the Torah if God will not forgive the people. God forgives Israel and reveals His thirteen attributes of mercy. Moses carves new tablets; God renews the covenant.

Significance for Noahides

The Golden Calf is the paradigmatic sin of idolatry — the first of the Seven Noahide Laws. Yet the story's greater lesson is about forgiveness: God's thirteen attributes of mercy — compassionate, gracious, slow to anger, abundant in kindness — are the foundation of Jewish prayer and the basis for hope in divine forgiveness.
