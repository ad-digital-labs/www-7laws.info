Bamidbar — Numbers

Forty years in the wilderness — the census, the journeys, and the trials of a nation being forged.

Bamidbar (בְּמִדְבַּר), meaning "In the wilderness," is the fourth book of the Torah. It covers the forty years of Israel's wandering in the desert — a period of testing, rebellion, divine punishment, and renewed faith. It includes the census of the tribes, the arrangement of the camp, and numerous episodes that reveal the human struggle to trust in God.

For Noahides, Bamidbar illustrates the consequences of moral failure and the power of repentance. The stories of Korach's rebellion, Balaam's prophecy, and the faithfulness of Pinchas offer timeless lessons about justice, humility, and the nature of divine providence.

Weekly Portions of Bamidbar

  • Bamidbar — The census of Israel, the arrangement of the camp around the Tabernacle
  • Naso — Duties of the Levites, the Sotah, the Nazirite, the Priestly Blessing
  • Behaalotecha — The menorah, the second Passover, the people's complaints, Miriam's leprosy
  • Shelach — The spies' report, the decree of forty years, the laws of challah and tzitzit
  • Korach — Korach's rebellion against Moses and Aaron, the earth swallows the rebels
  • Chukat — The red heifer, the death of Miriam and Aaron, the bronze serpent
  • Balak — Balak hires Balaam to curse Israel; Balaam blesses them instead
  • Pinchas — Pinchas' zealotry, the second census, the daughters of Zelophehad, Joshua appointed
  • Matot — Laws of vows, the war against Midian, the tribes of Reuben and Gad settle east of the Jordan
  • Masei — The journeys of Israel listed, the borders of the Land, cities of refuge
