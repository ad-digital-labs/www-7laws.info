Shoftim · Deuteronomy 16:18–21:9

Parasha Shoftim

Laws of judges, kings, priests, prophets, warfare, and cities of refuge — the foundations of a just society.

Overview

Parasha Shoftim is the Torah's comprehensive blueprint for a just society. It covers the appointment of judges, the laws of kingship, the role of the priests and Levites, the criteria for true prophecy, the laws of warfare, and the cities of refuge.

The Story

Moses commands: "Appoint judges and officers in all your gates... Justice, justice shall you pursue." The laws of a king follow: he must not accumulate too many horses, wives, or gold, and must write his own Torah scroll and read it all his life. The criteria for a true prophet: his words must come true, and he must not lead people to idolatry. Laws of warfare: offer peace before attacking, do not destroy fruit trees. The unsolved murder ritual — the eglah arufah — closes the parasha: when a body is found between cities, the elders of the nearest city must declare their innocence and pray for atonement.

Significance for Noahides

"Justice, justice shall you pursue" — the double emphasis on justice is the Torah's most direct statement of the Noahide obligation to establish courts. The laws of kingship teach that no human ruler is above the law — even the king must submit to the Torah. The criteria for true prophecy protect against false religious leaders who exploit their followers.

"Justice, justice shall you pursue, so that you may live and possess the land that the Lord your God is giving you." — Deuteronomy 16:20
