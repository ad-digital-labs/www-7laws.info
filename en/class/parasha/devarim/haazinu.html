Haazinu · Deuteronomy 32:1–32:52

Parasha Haazinu

Moses' great song — a poetic witness to God's faithfulness and Israel's destiny.

Overview

Parasha Haazinu consists almost entirely of the Song of Moses — a magnificent poem that serves as a prophetic witness to Israel's entire future history: their faithfulness, their straying, their punishment, and their ultimate redemption. It is one of the great poems of world literature.

The Story

Moses calls heaven and earth as witnesses and begins his song. He praises God's perfection and faithfulness, then describes Israel's ingratitude — how they grew fat and kicked, abandoned God, and provoked Him with foreign gods. God's anger burns; He hides His face. But He will not destroy them completely, lest the nations say "our hand prevailed." In the end, God will vindicate His people and take vengeance on their enemies: "See now that I, I am He, and there is no god beside Me." After the song, God tells Moses to ascend Mount Nebo and view the Land he will not enter.

Significance for Noahides

The Song of Haazinu is a compressed history of the world from the divine perspective — creation, covenant, faithfulness, failure, punishment, and redemption. Its central theological claim — that God is the only ultimate power, that all nations are under His sovereignty, and that history moves toward a final vindication of righteousness — is the foundation of Noahide faith and the hope of all who seek God.
