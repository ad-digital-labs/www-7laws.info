Vezot HaBracha · Deuteronomy 33:1–34:12

Parasha Vezot HaBracha

Moses blesses each tribe, ascends Mount Nebo, sees the Land, and the Torah comes to its end.

Overview

Parasha Vezot HaBracha — "And this is the blessing" — is the final parasha of the Torah, read on Simchat Torah when the annual cycle is completed and immediately begun again. It contains Moses' final blessings to each tribe and the account of his death — the most mysterious death in the Torah.

The Story

Moses blesses each of the twelve tribes with prophetic words describing their character and destiny. He opens with a vision of God's revelation at Sinai and closes with the declaration: "There is none like God, O Jeshurun, who rides the heavens to your help." Moses ascends Mount Nebo. God shows him the entire Land of Israel — from Dan to the Negev, from the Mediterranean to the Jordan Valley. God says: "This is the land I swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not cross over there." Moses dies there, in the land of Moab. God buries him; no one knows his burial place to this day. Moses was 120 years old; his eyes were undimmed and his vigor unabated. Israel wept for thirty days. The Torah closes: "Never again did there arise in Israel a prophet like Moses, whom God knew face to face."

Significance for Noahides

The Torah ends not with triumph but with a death — and yet it is a death of extraordinary dignity and mystery. Moses, the greatest human being who ever lived, dies alone with God on a mountaintop, his burial place unknown, so that no shrine could be built over his grave. The Torah's final words — "whom God knew face to face" — define the highest aspiration of human existence: to be known by God, to walk in His ways, and to leave the world better than you found it. This is the calling of every Noahide.
