Ki Tavo · Deuteronomy 26:1–29:8

Parasha Ki Tavo

First fruits, the declaration of tithes, the blessings on Gerizim and the curses on Ebal.

Overview

Parasha Ki Tavo describes the ceremony of bringing first fruits to the Temple — one of the Torah's most beautiful rituals — and then presents the great Tochachah of Devarim: an extended passage of blessings for obedience and devastating consequences for disobedience.

The Story

When Israel enters the Land, each person brings the first fruits of their harvest to the Temple and recites a declaration beginning: "My father was a wandering Aramean..." — a brief history of Israel from Jacob to the Exodus to the Promised Land. The declaration of tithes follows. Moses then commands the ceremony at Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal: six tribes stand on each mountain, the Levites pronounce twelve curses, and all the people respond "Amen." The great Tochachah follows — blessings for obedience and a long, harrowing passage of consequences for abandoning the covenant.

Significance for Noahides

The first fruits declaration — "My father was a wandering Aramean" — is a model of gratitude: acknowledging where you came from, what God did for you, and responding with an offering of your best. The Tochachah, while addressed to Israel, reflects a universal principle: moral choices have consequences, and the abandonment of God's ways leads to social and spiritual disintegration.
