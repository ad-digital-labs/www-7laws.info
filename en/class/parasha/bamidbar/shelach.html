Shelach · Numbers 13:1–15:41

Parasha Shelach

Twelve spies scout Canaan; ten bring a faithless report and doom a generation to the wilderness.

Overview

Parasha Shelach contains one of the most consequential failures in the Torah: the sin of the spies. Twelve leaders are sent to scout the Land of Canaan. Ten return with a report that paralyzes the people with fear. The result: an entire generation is condemned to die in the wilderness.

The Story

Moses sends twelve spies — one from each tribe — to scout Canaan for forty days. They return with enormous fruit and a mixed report: the land is indeed flowing with milk and honey, but the inhabitants are giants and the cities are fortified. Ten spies say: "We were like grasshoppers in our own eyes." Only Caleb and Joshua urge the people to trust God and go up. The people weep and speak of returning to Egypt. God is furious; Moses intercedes. God decrees: the entire generation that left Egypt will die in the wilderness over forty years — one year for each day of the spying. Only Caleb and Joshua will enter the Land.

Significance for Noahides

The sin of the spies was not cowardice but a failure of faith — they saw the obstacles and forgot the God who had split the sea and rained manna from heaven. The Talmud teaches that the night the people wept was the ninth of Av — a date that would become the most tragic in Jewish history. For Noahides, the parasha teaches that faithlessness in the face of divine promise has real consequences, and that the minority who trust God — like Caleb and Joshua — are ultimately vindicated.
