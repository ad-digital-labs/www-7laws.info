Pinchas · Numbers 25:10–30:1

Parasha Pinchas

Pinchas is rewarded for his zeal, a new census is taken, and Joshua is appointed as Moses' successor.

Overview

Parasha Pinchas opens with God's reward to Pinchas for his act of zealotry that stopped the plague, then moves to a second census of Israel, the case of the daughters of Zelophehad, the appointment of Joshua, and the laws of the festival offerings.

The Story

God grants Pinchas a covenant of peace and an eternal priesthood for his act of stopping the plague. A second census is taken — the new generation that will enter the Land. The daughters of Zelophehad approach Moses: their father died without sons; they ask for his inheritance. God rules in their favor — daughters inherit when there are no sons. Moses asks God to appoint a successor; God designates Joshua. Moses lays his hands on Joshua before the entire congregation. The parasha concludes with the detailed laws of the daily, Shabbat, and festival offerings.

Significance for Noahides

The daughters of Zelophehad model courageous advocacy for justice — they brought their case directly to Moses and to God, and were vindicated. Their story established a legal precedent that expanded women's rights in the Torah. The appointment of Joshua teaches the importance of orderly succession and the continuity of leadership in service of a divine mission.
