Matot · Numbers 30:2–32:42

Parasha Matot

The binding power of vows, the war against Midian, and two tribes choose to settle east of the Jordan.

Overview

Parasha Matot opens with the laws of vows — one of the most serious areas of Jewish law — then describes the war against Midian, and concludes with the tribes of Reuben and Gad requesting to settle in the fertile lands east of the Jordan River.

The Story

A man's vow is absolutely binding; a woman's vow may be annulled by her father or husband under certain conditions. Israel wages war against Midian as commanded — Balaam is killed. The spoils are divided between the warriors, the congregation, and the Levites. The tribes of Reuben and Gad, who have large flocks, ask to settle in the rich grazing lands of Gilead rather than cross into Canaan. Moses is initially angry — fearing a repeat of the spies' faithlessness — but agrees when they promise to fight alongside their brothers until the Land is conquered, then return to their portion east of the Jordan.

Significance for Noahides

The laws of vows teach that words have binding power — a principle directly relevant to the Noahide prohibition of theft and the broader ethic of honesty. "Let your yes be yes and your no be no." The negotiation between Moses and the tribes of Reuben and Gad models principled compromise: personal interests can be accommodated as long as communal obligations are fulfilled first.
