Masei · Numbers 33:1–36:13

Parasha Masei

The forty-two journeys of Israel listed, the borders of the Land defined, and cities of refuge established.

Overview

Parasha Masei closes the Book of Bamidbar by listing all forty-two encampments of Israel's wilderness journey, defining the borders of the Promised Land, and establishing the cities of refuge — a revolutionary institution of justice that distinguished between murder and manslaughter.

The Story

Moses records all forty-two stopping points of Israel's journey from Egypt to the plains of Moab — a sacred itinerary of the nation's formation. God defines the borders of Canaan and appoints leaders to oversee the land's division. Six cities of refuge are designated — three on each side of the Jordan — where a person who kills accidentally can flee from the blood avenger and receive a fair trial. Intentional murderers receive no refuge; accidental killers remain in the city until the High Priest dies. The book closes with the law that daughters who inherit must marry within their tribe to preserve tribal land boundaries.

Significance for Noahides

The cities of refuge embody the Noahide principle of establishing courts of justice. They distinguish between murder — a deliberate act deserving death — and manslaughter — an accidental act deserving protection and due process. This distinction is foundational to any just legal system. The listing of the forty-two journeys teaches that every stage of the journey — even the difficult ones — has a name and a purpose in God's plan.
