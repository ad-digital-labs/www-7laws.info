Korach · Numbers 16:1–18:32

Parasha Korach

Korach leads a rebellion against Moses and Aaron — and the earth swallows the rebels.

Overview

Parasha Korach describes the most serious internal rebellion in Israel's wilderness history. Korach, a Levite, together with Dathan, Abiram, and 250 leaders, challenges Moses and Aaron's authority. Their argument sounds democratic: "The entire congregation is holy — why do you raise yourselves above God's assembly?" But the rabbis identify it as a rebellion driven by jealousy and personal ambition, not genuine principle.

The Story

Moses falls on his face and proposes a test: each man will bring an incense offering before God, and God will show whom He has chosen. Moses warns the people to distance themselves from Korach's tents. The earth opens and swallows Korach, Dathan, Abiram, and their households. Fire consumes the 250 men offering incense. The next day, the people blame Moses for the deaths; a plague breaks out. Aaron runs with incense into the midst of the congregation and stops the plague. God then commands that Aaron's staff be placed in the Tabernacle — it sprouts, blossoms, and produces almonds overnight, confirming the Aaronic priesthood.

Significance for Noahides

Korach's rebellion illustrates the danger of using the language of equality and democracy to mask personal ambition and the desire for power. True leadership, the Torah teaches, is not self-appointed — it is divinely designated and confirmed. The establishment of courts and legitimate authority is one of the Seven Noahide Laws; Korach's challenge to legitimate authority is its antithesis.
