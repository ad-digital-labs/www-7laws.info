Naso · Numbers 4:21–7:89

Parasha Naso

The Priestly Blessing, the Nazirite vow, the Sotah, and the dedication offerings of the princes.

Overview

Parasha Naso is the longest single parasha in the Torah. It contains some of the most beloved passages in all of Scripture — including the Priestly Blessing — alongside laws of the Nazirite and the Sotah, and the lengthy account of the tribal leaders' dedication offerings.

The Story

The Levite clans receive their assignments. Laws of the Sotah (a woman suspected of adultery) and the Nazirite (one who vows to abstain from wine, haircuts, and contact with the dead) are given. Then comes the Priestly Blessing — the three-verse blessing Aaron and his sons are commanded to bestow upon Israel: "May God bless you and protect you; may God shine His face upon you and be gracious to you; may God turn His face toward you and grant you peace." The parasha concludes with twelve identical paragraphs describing the identical offerings brought by each tribal leader at the Tabernacle dedication.

Significance for Noahides

The Priestly Blessing is one of the oldest texts in the world — fragments were found on silver scrolls dating to the 7th century BCE. Its three-part structure — protection, grace, and peace — expresses the complete divine blessing available to all who seek God. The Nazirite teaches that voluntary self-discipline in service of a higher purpose is a legitimate and honored path.
