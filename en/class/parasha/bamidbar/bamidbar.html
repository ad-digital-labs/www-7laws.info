Bamidbar · Numbers 1:1–4:20

Parasha Bamidbar

God counts Israel by their tribes and arranges the camp around the Tabernacle.

Overview

Parasha Bamidbar opens the fourth book of the Torah with a census of the Israelite men of military age and a detailed description of how the twelve tribes were arranged around the Tabernacle in the wilderness. Order, structure, and each tribe's unique identity are the themes.

The Story

God commands Moses and Aaron to count all Israelite men aged twenty and above, tribe by tribe. The total is 603,550. The Levites are counted separately and assigned to carry and guard the Tabernacle. The twelve tribes are arranged in four groups of three around the Tabernacle — east, south, west, and north — each under its own banner. The Levite clans are assigned specific Tabernacle components to carry during the journeys.

Significance for Noahides

The census expresses the infinite value of each individual — God counts every person by name, not as a mass. The arrangement of the camp around the Tabernacle teaches that the divine presence should be at the center of communal life. Every tribe has its place, its banner, its identity — diversity within unity, all oriented toward the one God.
