Vayechi · Genesis 47:28–50:26

Parasha Vayechi

Jacob's final blessings, his death, and the close of the Book of Bereshit.

Overview

Parasha Vayechi — "And he lived" — is the final portion of Bereshit. Despite its name meaning life, it is a parasha of endings: Jacob's life ends, Joseph's life ends, and the patriarchal era closes. Yet it is also a parasha of blessing, prophecy, and the promise of future redemption.

The Story

Jacob, now 147 years old, summons Joseph and makes him swear to bury him in Canaan, in the Cave of Machpelah. He blesses Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, crossing his hands to give the younger Ephraim the greater blessing. He then gathers all twelve sons and delivers prophetic blessings to each, describing their characters and their futures. He instructs them to bury him with his fathers in Canaan and dies.

Joseph weeps over his father. Pharaoh grants permission for a great funeral procession to Canaan. Jacob is buried in Machpelah. The brothers, fearing Joseph's revenge now that Jacob is gone, send a message asking forgiveness. Joseph weeps and reassures them: "You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good." Joseph lives to 110, sees his great-grandchildren, and before he dies, makes the Israelites swear to carry his bones out of Egypt when God redeems them.

Significance for Noahides

Vayechi closes the book of Bereshit with a vision of blessing, continuity, and hope. Jacob's blessings to his sons are prophetic — they describe not just individuals but the destinies of nations. Joseph's final words — his insistence that his bones be taken to Canaan — express unshakeable faith in the divine promise of redemption. The entire book of Bereshit ends not with despair but with the seed of the Exodus already planted.
