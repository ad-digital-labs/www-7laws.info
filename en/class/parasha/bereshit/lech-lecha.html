Lech Lecha · Genesis 12:1–17:27

Parasha Lech Lecha

"Go for yourself" — God calls Abraham to leave everything and begin the journey of faith.

Overview

Parasha Lech Lecha introduces Abram (later Abraham) — the first person in history to recognize the one God through his own reasoning and to dedicate his life entirely to His service. God's call to Abraham marks the beginning of the Jewish people and the covenant that would shape all of history.

The Story

God commands Abram: "Go for yourself from your land, from your birthplace, and from your father's house, to the land that I will show you" (Genesis 12:1). God promises to make him a great nation, to bless him, and to make his name great. Abram obeys without question. In Canaan, God appears to Abram and promises the land to his descendants. A famine drives them to Egypt, where Abram passes Sarai off as his sister to protect himself. God afflicts Pharaoh's household, and they are sent away with great wealth. Back in Canaan, Abram and Lot separate due to conflict between their herdsmen. God reaffirms the promise of the land and descendants as numerous as the stars. Sarai gives her maidservant Hagar to Abram; Hagar bears Ishmael. God then establishes the covenant of circumcision with Abram, renames him Abraham, and renames Sarai to Sarah.

Significance for Noahides

Abraham is the model of the righteous Gentile who seeks God through reason and conscience. Before the Torah was given, before the Jewish people existed, Abraham recognized the one God and walked in His ways. For Noahides, Abraham's journey is an inspiration: the path of seeking God, leaving behind the idolatry of one's culture, and walking in faith toward a higher calling.
