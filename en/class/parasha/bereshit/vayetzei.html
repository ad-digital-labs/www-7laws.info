Vayetzei · Genesis 28:10–32:3

Parasha Vayetzei

Jacob flees to Haran, dreams of a heavenly ladder, and builds his family over twenty years.

Overview

Parasha Vayetzei follows Jacob as he leaves Canaan, fleeing Esau's wrath. On the way, he has a vision of a ladder reaching to heaven — one of the most iconic images in the Torah. He arrives at Laban's house, falls in love with Rachel, and spends twenty years working, being deceived, and ultimately building the twelve tribes of Israel.

The Story

Jacob stops for the night and dreams of a ladder with angels ascending and descending. God stands above it and reaffirms the Abrahamic covenant with Jacob. Jacob wakes and declares: "Surely God is in this place, and I did not know it." He vows that if God protects him, he will return in peace and give a tenth of all he has.

Jacob arrives in Haran and meets Rachel at a well. He rolls away the stone single-handedly — a feat of extraordinary strength — and weeps with joy. He works seven years for Rachel, but Laban tricks him into marrying Leah first. He works another seven years for Rachel. Through Leah, Rachel, and their maidservants Bilhah and Zilpah, Jacob fathers eleven sons and a daughter. After twenty years, Jacob secretly departs with his family and flocks. Laban pursues him; they make a covenant of peace at Gilead.

Significance for Noahides

Jacob's ladder is a universal symbol of the connection between heaven and earth — the idea that human actions have cosmic significance, and that God is present even in the most desolate places. Jacob's vow to tithe teaches the principle of gratitude and giving back to God from what one receives. His perseverance through Laban's deceptions models integrity under pressure.
