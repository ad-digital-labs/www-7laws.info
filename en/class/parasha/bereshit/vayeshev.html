Vayeshev · Genesis 37:1–40:23

Parasha Vayeshev

Joseph's dreams, his brothers' jealousy, and the beginning of his descent into Egypt.

Overview

Parasha Vayeshev begins the Joseph narrative — one of the most psychologically rich and spiritually layered stories in the entire Torah. Joseph, Jacob's beloved son, is gifted with prophetic dreams but despised by his brothers. His journey from the pit to Potiphar's house to prison is the beginning of a divine plan that will save the world.

The Story

Jacob loves Joseph more than his other sons and gives him a special coat. Joseph dreams that his brothers' sheaves bow to his, and that the sun, moon, and stars bow to him. His brothers hate him for his dreams and his father's favoritism. When Joseph comes to check on them in the field, they seize him, strip his coat, and throw him into a pit. They sell him to Ishmaelite traders for twenty pieces of silver. They dip his coat in goat's blood and show it to Jacob, who mourns inconsolably.

Joseph is brought to Egypt and sold to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh. God is with Joseph and he prospers. Potiphar's wife attempts to seduce him; he refuses, citing loyalty to his master and to God. She falsely accuses him and he is thrown into prison. Even there, God is with him — the prison warden puts Joseph in charge of all the prisoners.

Significance for Noahides

Joseph's story is a masterclass in moral integrity under pressure. He resists temptation not out of fear of punishment but out of loyalty to God and to his master. His suffering — unjust, prolonged, and seemingly without end — is the prelude to his greatness. The parasha teaches that God's providence works through human suffering, and that faithfulness in the pit is the preparation for the palace.
