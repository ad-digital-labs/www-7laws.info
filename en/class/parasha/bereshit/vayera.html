Vayera · Genesis 18:1–22:24

Parasha Vayera

Divine guests, the destruction of Sodom, and the ultimate test of Abraham's faith.

Overview

Parasha Vayera contains some of the most dramatic and theologically rich narratives in the entire Torah — from Abraham's hospitality to three divine visitors, to the destruction of Sodom, to the birth of Isaac, and finally to the Akeidah: the binding of Isaac on Mount Moriah.

The Story

Three angels appear to Abraham as men. Despite his old age and the heat of the day, Abraham rushes to welcome them with lavish hospitality. The angels announce that Sarah will bear a son within a year. Sarah laughs in disbelief. God reveals His plan to destroy Sodom and Gomorrah for their wickedness. Abraham boldly intercedes, bargaining with God to spare the cities if righteous people can be found. The angels go to Sodom; the men of Sodom surround Lot's house demanding the guests be handed over. The angels blind the attackers and warn Lot to flee. Fire and brimstone rain down on Sodom and Gomorrah. Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt. Sarah conceives and bears Isaac. Hagar and Ishmael are sent away. Then comes the supreme test: God commands Abraham to offer Isaac as a burnt offering. Abraham obeys without hesitation. At the last moment, an angel stops him — a ram is offered instead. God swears to bless Abraham and his descendants because of his faithfulness.

Significance for Noahides

The destruction of Sodom illustrates the Noahide prohibition of injustice. Sodom's sin was the institutionalization of cruelty and the rejection of hospitality and compassion. Abraham's intercession shows that righteous individuals can and should advocate for justice before God. The Akeidah teaches that true righteousness sometimes demands the surrender of what is most precious to us, trusting that God's ways are ultimately good.
