Vayishlach · Genesis 32:4–36:43

Parasha Vayishlach

Jacob wrestles with the divine, is renamed Israel, and reunites with his brother Esau.

Overview

Parasha Vayishlach is a parasha of transformation. Jacob returns to Canaan after twenty years, faces the terrifying prospect of meeting Esau with four hundred men, and undergoes a mysterious nighttime struggle that changes his name — and his destiny — forever.

The Story

Jacob sends messengers ahead to Esau with gifts and words of peace. He learns Esau is coming with four hundred men and is afraid. He divides his camp, prays to God, and sends wave after wave of gifts. That night, alone by the Jabbok river, a man wrestles with him until dawn. Jacob refuses to let go until the man blesses him. The man asks his name; Jacob says "Jacob." The man says: "Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel — for you have striven with God and with men and have prevailed." Jacob limps away, marked by the struggle. The sciatic nerve is forbidden to eat in memory of this event.

Jacob and Esau meet and embrace. Esau offers to travel together; Jacob declines gently and they part in peace. Jacob arrives in Shechem. His daughter Dinah is violated by Shechem; her brothers Simeon and Levi take violent revenge. Jacob rebukes them. Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin. Isaac dies; Jacob and Esau bury him together.

Significance for Noahides

Jacob's wrestling match is one of the Torah's most profound metaphors: the struggle of the human soul with the divine, with conscience, with one's own nature. The name "Israel" — one who strives with God — defines the Jewish people's relationship with the divine as one of active engagement, not passive submission. For Noahides, it models the honest, wrestling quality of genuine faith.
