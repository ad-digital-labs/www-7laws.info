Vayigash · Genesis 44:18–47:27

Parasha Vayigash

Judah's plea, Joseph's revelation, and the reunion of a family torn apart by jealousy.

Overview

Parasha Vayigash contains one of the most emotionally powerful moments in the Torah: Joseph's revelation to his brothers. After years of separation, suffering, and divine orchestration, the family is reunited — and Joseph reframes all his suffering as part of God's plan.

The Story

Judah steps forward and delivers a passionate plea on Benjamin's behalf, offering himself as a slave in Benjamin's place. Joseph can no longer contain himself. He sends his servants out and weeps so loudly that the Egyptians hear him. He reveals himself: "I am Joseph. Is my father still alive?" His brothers are stunned and cannot answer. Joseph embraces Benjamin and weeps; he kisses all his brothers.

He tells them: "Do not be distressed or angry with yourselves for selling me here, for God sent me ahead of you to preserve life." Pharaoh invites the entire family to settle in Egypt. Jacob is overcome with joy when he hears Joseph is alive. The family — seventy souls — descends to Egypt and settles in Goshen. Joseph presents his father to Pharaoh; Jacob blesses Pharaoh.

Significance for Noahides

Joseph's forgiveness of his brothers is one of the Torah's greatest teachings on forgiveness and divine providence. He does not minimize what they did — he reframes it: God used their evil for a greater good. This is not naive optimism but deep theological insight: that God's providence works through human choices, even sinful ones, to accomplish His purposes. For Noahides, it is a model of how to process suffering and extend forgiveness.
