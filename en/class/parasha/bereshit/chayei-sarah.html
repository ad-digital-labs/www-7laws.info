Chayei Sarah · Genesis 23:1–25:18

Parasha Chayei Sarah

The life of Sarah ends, and the covenant continues through Isaac and Rebecca.

Overview

Parasha Chayei Sarah — "The Life of Sarah" — opens with Sarah's death and closes with Abraham's death, yet its central theme is continuity: the covenant must pass to the next generation. Abraham's faithful servant Eliezer undertakes the mission of finding a wife for Isaac, and the story of Rebecca's selection is one of the most beautifully told in the Torah.

The Story

Sarah dies at the age of 127 in Hebron. Abraham mourns her and negotiates with the Hittites to purchase the Cave of Machpelah as a burial site — the first piece of the Promised Land to be legally acquired by the patriarchs. Abraham then sends his servant Eliezer to his homeland to find a wife for Isaac from among his own family. Eliezer prays for a sign: the woman who offers water to him and his camels will be the one. Before he finishes praying, Rebecca appears and does exactly that. Eliezer gives her gifts and meets her family. Rebecca agrees to go with him. She and Isaac meet in the field at evening; Isaac brings her into his mother's tent and loves her. Abraham later takes another wife, Keturah, has more children, and dies at 175, buried beside Sarah in Machpelah.

Significance for Noahides

This parasha teaches the importance of prayer, trust in divine providence, and the sanctity of marriage. Eliezer's prayer — direct, specific, and immediately answered — is a model of sincere communication with God. The story of Rebecca's kindness to a stranger and his animals reflects the universal values of compassion and generosity that are at the heart of a righteous life.
