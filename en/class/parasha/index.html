What is the Torah?

The Torah (תּוֹרָה) is the foundational text of Judaism — the Five Books of Moses given by God to the Jewish people at Mount Sinai. The word Torah means "instruction" or "teaching." It contains the laws, narratives, and divine wisdom that form the basis of Jewish life and the moral framework for all humanity.

The Torah is divided into five books, known collectively as the Chumash (חוּמָשׁ), from the Hebrew word for "five." Each book covers a distinct era of sacred history — from the creation of the world to the eve of the Jewish people's entry into the Land of Israel.

What is a Parasha?

The Torah is divided into 54 weekly portions, called parashot (פָּרָשִׁיּוֹת; singular: parasha). Each Shabbat, Jewish communities around the world read one parasha aloud from a handwritten Torah scroll. Over the course of a year, the entire Torah is completed and the cycle begins again.

The word parasha means "portion" or "section." Each portion has a name taken from its opening words, and carries its own themes, laws, stories, and spiritual lessons.

Significance for Jews and Righteous Gentiles

For Jews, the weekly parasha is the heartbeat of Jewish life. It structures the year, anchors communal worship, and provides an endless source of study, commentary, and spiritual growth. The Torah is not merely history — it is a living document, studied and applied anew in every generation.

For Righteous Gentiles (Noahides), the Torah holds profound significance as well. The Seven Noahide Laws are rooted in the Torah and were transmitted through Moses at Sinai. Studying the Torah — even without being obligated in all its commandments — deepens one's understanding of God's will, the nature of creation, and the moral foundations of a righteous life.

The Talmud teaches that a non-Jew who studies the parts of Torah relevant to them is considered like a High Priest. The stories of the patriarchs, the narratives of creation, the covenant with Noah, and the ethical teachings woven throughout the Torah are a universal inheritance — a gift from God to all of humanity.

"The Torah was given publicly, in the open, in a place belonging to no one — so that anyone who wishes to receive it may come and receive it." — Mechilta, Yitro

The Five Books of Moses (Chumash)

Select a book to explore its description and weekly Torah portions:

  • Bereshit (Genesis) — The book of beginnings: creation, the patriarchs, and the descent to Egypt
  • Shemot (Exodus) — The redemption from Egypt, the giving of the Torah at Sinai, and the Tabernacle
  • Vayikra (Leviticus) — The laws of the priesthood, sacrifices, purity, and holiness
  • Bamidbar (Numbers) — The journeys of Israel in the wilderness and the challenges of faith
  • Devarim (Deuteronomy) — Moses' final addresses, a review of the Torah, and his farewell to Israel
