Law 7 of 7

Establishment Honest and Moral Government and Justice System

Uphold law, order, and justice in society.

Overview

The seventh Noahide Law is unique among the seven: it is a positive commandment. Rather than prohibiting a harmful act, it obligates society to create and maintain functioning systems of justice. Courts must be established to enforce the other six laws and to adjudicate disputes fairly.

What It Requires

  • Establishing courts of law to judge and enforce the Noahide Laws
  • Appointing honest and competent judges
  • Ensuring equal and impartial treatment under the law
  • Punishing those who violate the other six laws
  • Creating a legal system that protects the rights of all members of society

Deeper Understanding

Without courts and enforcement, the other six laws remain ideals without teeth. Justice must be institutionalized — embedded in the structures of society — to have real effect. This is why the seventh law is considered the social foundation upon which all the others rest.

The Talmud teaches that this obligation applies to every nation and society. Each community is responsible for building just institutions — not merely hoping that individuals will behave well, but actively creating systems that reward righteousness and penalize wrongdoing.

Practical Observance

A Noahide fulfills this law by supporting just governance, participating in civic life, advocating for fair laws, and holding authorities accountable. On a personal level, it means resolving disputes through legitimate channels and refusing to take justice into one's own hands.
