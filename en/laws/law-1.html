Law 1 of 7

Belief in One G-d

Do not worship idols or false deities.

Overview

The first of the Noahide Laws is the prohibition against idolatry — the worship of any being, object, or force other than the one true God. This law is not merely a ritual requirement; it is the foundation upon which all moral and spiritual life is built.

What It Prohibits

  • Worshipping statues, images, or physical representations as divine
  • Offering prayer, sacrifice, or devotion to any being other than God
  • Believing in or serving multiple gods (polytheism)
  • Attributing divine power to created things such as the sun, stars, or natural forces

Deeper Understanding

Idolatry distorts the human relationship with the Creator. When a person directs their ultimate devotion toward anything finite — wealth, power, pleasure, or ideology — they are, in essence, committing a form of idolatry. True monotheism demands that God alone be recognized as the source of all existence and the ultimate authority over all creation.

The Talmud teaches that rejecting idolatry is equivalent to accepting the entire Torah. For non-Jews, this begins with recognizing and affirming the oneness of God as an intellectual and spiritual conviction, not merely an absence of idol worship.

Practical Observance

A Noahide should direct all prayer and religious devotion solely to God. One should avoid participating in religious rites that involve the worship of any intermediary as a deity, and should cultivate an ongoing awareness of God's presence and sovereignty in daily life.
